# Which Edging Play to Pick

The edging series currently has three plays, all built on the air-pressure sensor of the edging combo device (CUNZHI01): the device tracks arousal, stops stimulation (or fires a shock penalty) at the edge, then starts the next round after a cooldown. The differences are mainly the trigger mechanism and the setup required.

| Play | Trigger | Pressure setup | Difficulty | Best for |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| [Built-in edging](/docs/player/cunzhi01) | Stops automatically when pressure reaches the edging threshold | Set the edging threshold | Simplest — just the host and you're playing | First time with an edging device |
| [3-phase air-pressure edging](/docs/player/cunzhi3step) | Reaching the critical pressure triggers a shock and enters the edge phase | Set middle pressure and critical pressure manually | Needs a hotspot and Wi-Fi connection — relatively involved | Players who want finer control or to link devices like the auto lock |
| [Pressure-surge edging](/docs/player/surge-edging) | Triggers when pressure rises by the threshold within a short window | Middle pressure auto-calibrates every round | Needs networking and client configuration | Players who struggle to tune a fixed threshold or are affected by slow baseline drift |

## Short answer

- **Just want to try it quickly**: use the built-in edging. Install the client, connect the device, and play. A "tiptoe + Kegel" mode on the same host also does posture training on the side (see [Tiptoe and Kegel tutorial](/docs/player/tiptoe-kegel)).
- **Want precise manual control**: use the 3-phase version. The calm → middle → edge → cooldown cycle is driven entirely by your middle and critical pressure settings, and it supports linking an auto lock, OSR6, and other devices.
- **Can't get a manual threshold right**: use the pressure-surge version. It triggers on a short-window pressure rise and recalibrates middle pressure every round, reducing how much slow leaks and other baseline drift break a fixed threshold.

Assembly and wearing are identical across all three plays — see [Assembly and wearing](/docs/device/cunzhi01/assembly).
